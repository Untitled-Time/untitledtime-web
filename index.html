EST. 2026 — WILLIAM & MARY

Untitled Time

A vessel that keeps no clock time.

It holds three times at once — the deep time of its fossil stone, the brief time of the face it reflects, and the indeterminate transit time of a stone carried away by a stranger's hands.

01. THE EXIT

火 The White Obelisk

It is February 8th, 2026. On Launch Pad 39B, the Artemis II rocket stands as a white obelisk against the Florida sky. Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen are preparing to loop around the Moon—the first leg of a relay race that ends on Mars. It is a machine built for a singular, ancient purpose: escape.

"We are leaving home because we no longer want to sit still."

Whether the fire lights today or in March, the exit is inevitable. We are building these ships because the human mandate has always been to expand, to push outward, to leave the cradle. We are launching into the void because we have forgotten the art of staying.

02. THE OBSOLESCENCE

金 The Silicon Silence

But while we build engines to leave Earth, a terrifying quiet is spreading across it. The predictions made at Bletchley Park have arrived: the Machine—cold, efficient, and metallic—can now code, diagnose, and create faster than any human hand. You are not merely "unemployed"; you are rendered obsolete.

In a world where work, the traditional source of human dignity, is vanished, we are left with a haunting vacuum. We face a future where our only remaining value might be to serve as content for the machine to observe.

"If you do not need to survive, how do you find a reason to live?"
03. THE SEED

木 The Cathedral of Patience

The answer to the machine is not more silicon. It is the Seed. Walk into the Nordmarka forest outside Oslo. Here, the artist Katie Paterson has planted 1,000 spruce saplings. They are small, fragile, and utterly silent.

They will not be harvested for one hundred years. When they are cut in 2114, they will become the paper for an anthology of books that no one living today will ever read. This is the Anti-Rocket. It asks the most difficult question of all: Can you plant a seed for a shadow you will never see?

04. THE MIRROR

水 The Mirror

Before glass, the first mirror was still water. You look down and meet yourself — and nothing else.

上善若水: the highest good is like water. It nourishes all things and does not contend (不争); it flows to the lowest places, and so becomes the sea.

In the Heart Sutra, the five aggregates of the self are empty — 五蕴皆空.

The vessel is built from this: a bowl that holds no water, drilled through so rain and light pass and nothing gathers — and at its base, a mirror, returning your face.

宇宙就是镜子. Time here is not measured but reflected; not read off a face, but sought within.

05. THE VESSEL

土 The Vessel

A stone bowl rests in a forest. Carved from Coquina — Virginia fossil stone, ~2.5 million years old — it holds nothing. Behind a field of drainage holes, a convex mirror returns your face, brief and curved, against deep time. The gap between you and your reflection is the charged distance Michelangelo left between the fingers of Adam and God: the spark lives because they do not touch.

A second vessel stands at the University of St Andrews, cut from 330-million-year-old Carboniferous limestone — each site's own stone becomes its own clock. Each vessel exists at two heights: one at adult eye level, one lowered to a child's.

You may lift a stone from the vessel and set it down elsewhere — across the clearing, across an ocean, or never. That indeterminate journey is the only unit this clock keeps.

06. THE SYSTEM

北斗 The Walking Constellation

College Woods becomes a ground map of the Big Dipper: seven pauses along a trail, each vessel a point of attention rather than a monument. The visitor does not read a clock. They walk a constellation slowly enough for time to change scale.

Across the Atlantic, St Andrews answers with its own stone and its own weather. William & Mary and St Andrews are not set against one another; they become two historical universities holding a quiet correspondence in deep time.
